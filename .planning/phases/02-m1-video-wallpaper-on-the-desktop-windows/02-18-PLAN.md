## Phase Goal

ROADMAP goal: *An existing user's selected video theme plays behind the desktop icons on every monitor on Windows through libmpv, survives shell and monitor changes, and pauses for fullscreen apps.*

This plan's slice (UAT gap G-02-2c, first half): **plugging a monitor adds a renderer without restarting playback on the others.** The second half (a follower that really reaches the same frame) is plan 02-19.

<objective>
Stop the master election from picking a renderer that is not playing yet, stop clocks before the first frame, and make the master policy on a primary-monitor change explicit, with a maintainer decision on that policy.

Purpose: in UAT (19:02:26Z) the re-enabled virtual display came back as primary, its brand-new renderer was elected master before it said Hello, published position 0.0002 as a running clock, and the renderer that had played for 8.5 min seeked back to the start. Root cause: `.planning/debug/monitor-plug-restarts-playback-and-frame-offset.md`.
Output: `PlayerEvent::Started`, Playing-after-first-frame, playing-only election, `choose_master` policy with tests, FEATURES note.

Decision flag: the orchestrator listed master-clock policy changes as not yet decided. Option A (sticky master) matches the 02-CONTEXT discretion wording "the primary monitor, with another renderer promoted when the master's monitor goes away" and changes no legacy-facing behavior (the legacy app has no master clock, and audio goes to the same default device whichever renderer plays it), but it does change the M1 note in docs/FEATURES.md, so Task 2 asks the maintainer before any policy code is written. Task 1 is needed by both options.
</objective>

<task type="checkpoint:decision" gate="blocking">
  <name>Task 2: Maintainer chooses the master policy for a primary-monitor change</name>
  <decision>When the primary monitor changes while renderers play (a monitor plugged in as the new primary, or Windows switching the primary), which renderer is the master clock?</decision>
  <context>Task 1 already stops a renderer that is not playing from becoming master. Once the new primary's renderer plays, today's stateless rule still moves the master to it, and the renderer that was master is demoted and seeks to the newcomer's position (a visible jump). The master only matters for sync and for which process plays the audio; the audio goes to the same default output device either way, and the legacy C# app has no master clock, so neither option changes a legacy behavior. 02-CONTEXT (Claude's discretion) says "the primary monitor, with another renderer promoted when the master's monitor goes away". docs/FEATURES.md currently says monitors "follow the primary monitor's renderer".</context>
  <options>
    <option id="sticky">
      <name>A. Sticky master (recommended)</name>
      <pros>Matches the 02-CONTEXT wording; the master stays the renderer that is already playing as long as its monitor exists and it plays, so a plug never moves the clock or the audio and never makes anyone jump; small change (one pure function and tests in service.rs); no protocol change.</pros>
      <cons>After a primary change the master may sit on a non-primary monitor until its monitor goes away; the FEATURES note changes from "the primary monitor's renderer" to "the first playing renderer, primary first at start".</cons>
    </option>
    <option id="handover">
      <name>B. Primary wins after a handover</name>
      <pros>Keeps "the primary monitor's renderer is the master" as documented; the new primary's renderer first follows, and takes over only once it is aligned, so nobody jumps.</pros>
      <cons>More moving parts: a new ToService::Aligned message (appended), follower-side alignment detection (3 consecutive ticks within one frame), and a handover step in the service; the role and the audio move once per primary change.</cons>
    </option>
  </options>
  <resume-signal>Reply "sticky" or "handover".</resume-signal>
